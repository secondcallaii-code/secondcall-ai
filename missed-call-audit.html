Prefer to check your real line? The free self-audit

The scan works from the numbers you entered. These three checks take about ten minutes tonight and test the estimate against your actual phone line.

1

Call your own line after hours

After closing, call your business number from a phone your team does not normally use. Do not judge it as the owner — judge it as a customer with a problem. How long does it ring? Does anyone answer? If it goes to voicemail, how clear is the next step? Write down exactly what the caller gets, because that experience is your front desk after dark.

2

Read last month’s call log

Look for numbers that called and never connected. Patterns matter more than the raw count: repeated calls from the same number, rings during lunch, or calls that landed while everyone was already on a job. Pick two numbers and call them back. Ask what they needed and whether they found help. Keep it simple and take the answer at face value.

3

Count the rings nobody hears

For one week, keep a plain tally of calls that come in after closing and on weekends. Use your phone system’s records if you have them; if not, ask your provider what the line captured. You are not building a report. You are answering one question: when your shop is closed, how often is a customer still trying to reach you?

What the tally is really telling you

If those checks turn up only wrong numbers and sales calls, your line is doing its job. If even one was a real customer trying to book work, the audit found the gap. The fix is not answering faster when you are already busy. The fix is making sure a caller always reaches someone who can help — including when you and your crew cannot pick up.

Industry reporting puts the share of calls to local shops that go unanswered at 20–30%. That does not mean every missed call was a ready-to-book job — some are suppliers, robocalls, or people dialing the wrong number. But some are exactly the calls you advertise for, and when those callers get silence, they typically dial the next business on Google.
